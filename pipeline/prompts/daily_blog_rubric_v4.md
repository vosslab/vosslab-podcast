# Daily maker blog rubric

After reading this post, does it feel like Neil sat down after coding and wrote about what he
made, what interested or surprised him, why he enjoyed working on it, what he learned, and what
he wants to try next?

Score each criterion from 1 through 4, then use the listed weights to guide the overall choice.
Mechanical publication checks belong to the editorial validator.

## Maker substance (25%)

- 4: Centers a specific artifact, change, investigation, or decision from today's software work
  and explains its technical problem, movement, and practical meaning.
- 3: Centers real work and explains a concrete change with useful technical context.
- 2: Presents several real changes with a limited account of their technical connection.
- 1: Presents a broad activity summary with sparse artifact-level explanation.

## Author presence and curiosity (20%)

- 4: Shows the maker noticing, reasoning, reacting, and revising through several specific
  moments that reveal a recognizable person at work.
- 3: Shows a clear first-person reaction or line of reasoning connected to the work.
- 2: Includes occasional first-person framing alongside predominantly report-like description.
- 1: Uses first-person framing mainly as a record of completed activity.

## Insight and selectivity (20%)

- 4: Develops one especially meaningful thread, explains why it earned attention, and gives
  routine work economical supporting space.
- 3: Gives the strongest development a clear priority and explains why it matters.
- 2: Groups related developments with an emerging priority.
- 1: Gives developments largely equal space and leaves their relative importance implicit.

## Concrete technical grounding (15%)

- 4: Uses precise implementation details, examples, links, measurements, or artifacts that make
  the maker's reasoning and result easy to understand.
- 3: Uses several concrete technical details that clarify the described change.
- 2: Uses a small set of technical details with partial explanatory value.
- 1: Uses general technical language with limited connection to a specific implementation.

## Narrative and readability (10%)

- 4: Carries the reader through a natural movement from attention or problem to exploration and
  current state with clean, economical prose.
- 3: Presents a coherent progression with readable paragraphs and useful transitions.
- 2: Presents an understandable sequence with some list-like movement.
- 1: Presents a sequence of updates with a lightly developed narrative thread.

## Intellectual honesty and unfinished edges (10%)

- 4: Distinguishes confirmed progress from active uncertainty, names a meaningful frontier, and
  ends at the point where the maker's thinking currently stands.
- 3: Names a genuine question, trade-off, or next experiment connected to the work.
- 2: Mentions a future direction with limited connection to the preceding account.
- 1: Closes with a broad future intention or completion statement.
